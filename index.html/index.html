The problem

Inaccessible design shuts people out of school, work and public services.

When a button has no label, a video has no captions or a form needs a mouse, a person is stopped. It is rarely on purpose. It is usually because nobody tested for it.

1 in 6people worldwide live with a significant disability (about 1.3 billion).
~95%of the top one million home pages have detectable accessibility failures.
1 in 12men have some form of colour vision deficiency.

Sources: World Health Organization, Global report on health equity for persons with disabilities (2022); WebAIM Million annual analysis; Colour Blind Awareness.

Barriers and fixes

Different people hit different walls.

What goes wrong

  • Images with no text description are invisible to screen readers.
  • Light grey text on white cannot be read by people with low vision.
  • Status shown only by red or green is lost to colour-blind users.
  • Pages that break when zoomed to 200%.

What fixes it

  • Write meaningful alt text, and empty alt for decoration.
  • Keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or higher.
  • Add text or shapes next to colour.
  • Use relative units so text resizes.

What goes wrong

  • Videos without captions.
  • Audio-only alerts and voice-only help lines.
  • Sign language users facing long written forms with no plain-language option.

What fixes it

  • Add accurate captions and transcripts.
  • Show a visual cue for every sound.
  • Offer chat, text or email beside phone support.

What goes wrong

  • Menus that only open on mouse hover.
  • Tiny tap targets that are hard to hit with a tremor.
  • Timed forms that expire before someone using a switch device can finish.

What fixes it

  • Make everything work with the keyboard, in a logical order.
  • Keep targets at least 44 by 44 pixels.
  • Let people extend or turn off time limits.

What goes wrong

  • Dense paragraphs and jargon.
  • Flashing or auto-playing content that pulls attention away or triggers seizures.
  • Error messages that say "Invalid input" and nothing more.

What fixes it

  • Use short sentences and plain words.
  • Give people control: pause, stop, hide motion.
  • Say what went wrong and how to fix it.
